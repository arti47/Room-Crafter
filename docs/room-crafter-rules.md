# The Room Crafter — Rules Reference

Source: *Mythic Magazine* Volume 69, "Variations: Single Room Explorations".
Inspired by The Location Crafter, adapted for tight spaces. Terminology is
shared with LC: the **room is the Region**; the features inside it are **Areas**.

## 1. Scope: what counts as a "room"

Any small, personal space varied enough to allow exploration, whose contents
can be taken in at a glance and reached in a moment.

- Bounded: living room, bedroom, dungeon chamber, starship crew quarters,
  shallow cave, car interior, large pond.
- Unbounded but conceptually contained: forest clearing, sea-shore grotto,
  ring of standing stones — limited by what the space means to the PC.
- Multi-room shorthand: a simple apartment (living room + kitchen + bedroom +
  bath) may be treated as one "room" if a thirty-second walkthrough reveals it.
  Place generated Areas wherever they fit best (bookcase in the living room,
  dresser in the bedroom).

Unlike LC, Areas are not discovered one at a time by entering them — all
visible Areas are known immediately, and they double as the room's description.

## 2. Generating the room (explorable Areas)

1. Roll **six Keywords** on the Room Descriptors Meaning table, one at a time,
   slowly.
2. If a Keyword suggests an easy interpretation, make it an explorable Area.
3. If not, roll the next Keyword and combine it with the previous one.
4. Continue in order until all six Keywords are consumed. If the last Keyword
   yields nothing, drop it.

Result: **3–6 explorable Areas** (a room has at most six).

Variant — faster rooms (dungeon crawls, many rooms): use **three Keywords**,
giving 2–3 Areas (3–4 including the General Area).

### Interpreting

The Areas are the room's main features; describe the room from them, then
embellish with whatever the Context makes expected (a window with curtains,
boots against a wall). Embellishments are **not** explorable Areas.

Don't dwell. Stretches are fine. You need only enough to know a thing is
there — the equivalent of a GM reading a room summary from a module. Detail
comes later from inspecting elements.

There is no obligation to search anything; the description alone may be all
you need.

## 4. Searching the room

Any identified Area may be searched. **Roll once per Area** on the Room
Elements table. There is no Conclusion Element — the room is complete once
every Area plus the General Area has been checked.

### Room Elements (d100)

| d100 | Element |
|---|---|
| 1–40 | **Expected** — it is what it appears to be / contains what you expect. |
| 41–50 | **Enhanced Expected** — as expected, but more: more valuable, more important. |
| 51–65 | **Minimized Expected** — as expected, but lesser: less valuable, less important. |
| 66–70 | **Fortunate** — a boon for the PC. Use the obvious idea; otherwise Fate Questions / Discover Meaning. |
| 71–80 | **Unfortunate** — something bad for the PC. Same fallback. |
| 81–95 | **Random** — choose a Meaning table, roll a Keyword pair, interpret. |
| 96–100 | **Multi-Element** — roll until you have two *separate* Elements. A repeat or another Multi-Element is treated as Expected. |

### What "expected" means

Base expectations on the **perceived object at face value**, never on what the
PC or Player wants. Looking under a bed in a bedroom yields bed-adjacent
things (a pair of shoes), not the sought-after grimoire — desires are resolved
by **Fortunate / Unfortunate**, which key off narrative good/bad rather than
face value. **Random** is the wildcard and may produce anything.

## 5. The General Area

One extra explorable Area: the room itself — everything not immediately
noticeable. A general poke-around for anything out of the ordinary.

- Exactly **one** roll on Room Elements.
- Interpret consistently with the room as already known.

Totals: default six Keywords → 4–7 explorable Areas including the General
Area; three Keywords → 3–4.

## 6. Encounters

The Room Crafter covers the room and its contents only, not active elements.
Generate Areas and the description **first**, then ask the Fate Question
"Is there an encounter?"

| Answer | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Exceptional Yes | An encounter that is important or special. Use what you most expect, or Discover Meaning. |
| Yes | An encounter. Use what you most expect, or Discover Meaning. |
| No / Exceptional No | No immediate encounter. |

If the Fate Question isn't enough, Discover Meaning on a table such as Actions.

## 7. Hidden things and ability-based searches

The Room Crafter reports what is apparent to the eye. For concealed things
(secret doors, hidden objects):

1. Optionally resolve a search task with your RPG's mechanics (estimate
   difficulty).
2. On success — or if not using task mechanics — ask the Fate Question
   "Is something hidden found?", assigning Odds as you see fit.
3. If found, use Fate Questions or Discover Meaning to identify it.

## 8. Dungeon crawls

Chain rooms back to back to generate a crawl. Use fewer Keywords (three) per
room for speed; "room" may be a cave, corridor, cavern, or pool. Combined with
"Is there an encounter?" and Discovering Meaning, this is a complete random
dungeon generator.

## 10. Procedure summary

```
1. Enter room.
2. Roll 6 (or 3) Room Descriptor Keywords, sequentially.
   - single Keyword inspires -> Area
   - otherwise combine with next Keyword -> Area
   -> 3-6 Areas (2-3 at three Keywords)
3. Describe the room from the Areas + Context; embellish freely
   (embellishments are not Areas).
4. Fate Question: "Is there an encounter?" -> resolve if Yes.
5. For each Area the PC searches: 1 roll on Room Elements.
   - Multi-Element -> two distinct Elements
   - Random -> Meaning-table Keyword pair
   - Fortunate/Unfortunate -> obvious idea, else Fate/Meaning
6. Optional: hidden-item searches via task mechanic +
   "Is something hidden found?"
7. General Area: 1 roll on Room Elements.
8. Room complete.
```

## 11. Worked examples in the source text

- **Helen, St. Augustine 1586** (six Keywords: Symbol, Sparse, Crammed,
  Pristine, Medicine, Busy) → flag on the wall, cramped bed, tidy shelf of
  tonics, jumbled crates. Searches: bed = Minimized Expected (shoes);
  shelf = Multi-Element (Random "Enemy, Leave" → poison + Expected tonics);
  crates = Expected (sailor's curios); flag = Enhanced Expected (hidden letter,
  Discover Meaning "Guides, Disadvantage"). General Area = Fortunate → the
  grimoire under a loose floorboard.
- **Bunker 23, Officer Zekus's office** (Device, Information, Storage, Wall,
  Symbol, Furniture) → computer terminal, file cabinet, chalkboard, cluttered
  desk. Searches: terminal = Expected; chalkboard = Multi-Element
  (Unfortunate "Create, Value" → breach schematic for Bunker 22, + Expected);
  desk = Expected; cabinet = Fortunate ("Carry, Health" → disease records and
  Bunker 19's cure offer). General Area = Random ("Abandon, Expectation") →
  Zekus's discarded Security Officer badge.
